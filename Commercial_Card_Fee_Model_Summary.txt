Commercial + Small Business Card Fee Overview
Model Summary

DEVELOPMENT DATA

Monthly Commercial Card and Small Business fee revenue and macroeconomic data
from Jan 2011 to Dec 2025.

In-sample (IS): Jan 2011-Sep 2023, 153 months.
Out-of-time (OOT): Oct 2023-Dec 2025, 27 months.

METHODOLOGY

1. Target variable
   Combined fee revenue = Commercial Card + Small Business.
   The short-run model forecasts its YoY growth:

   y_t = 100 x (Combined_t / Combined_{t-12} - 1)

2. Long-run relationship
   Estimate combined fee revenue as a function of CPI using IS data.

3. Error correction and overlay
   Express the long-run residual as a percentage of the contemporaneous fitted
   level. Subtract a linear trend estimated over the 27-month OOT window to
   obtain the adjusted error-correction term.

4. Short-run model
   Apply IS/OOT macro screening and forward selection. The final model includes
   adjusted EC at lag 12, own YoY growth at lags 1 and 6, and corporate profit
   YoY growth at lag 2.

5. Scenario forecasts
   Recursively forecast monthly fee revenue from Jan 2026 to Mar 2028 using
   baseline and S4 CPI and corporate profit paths. Convert predicted YoY growth
   into fee levels and update lagged inputs each month.

ASSUMPTIONS

- Estimated model relationships remain stable over the forecast horizon.
- The overlay remains fixed at its Dec 2025 value of -30.143 percentage points
  of scaled EC, without further trend extrapolation.
- Baseline and S4 use the same coefficients and historical starting point.
  Differences reflect the supplied macroeconomic scenario paths.

LIMITATIONS & WEAKNESSES

- OOT observations inform variable screening and overlay estimation, so
  reported OOT errors represent a fitted backtest rather than fully independent
  validation.
- The CPI-based long-run relationship and fixed overlay may not capture
  further structural changes in fee generation.
- Recursive forecasts can accumulate errors. Reported point forecasts do not
  quantify parameter or macroeconomic scenario uncertainty.
