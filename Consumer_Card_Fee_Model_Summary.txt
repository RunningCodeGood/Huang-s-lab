Consumer Card Fee Overview
Model Summary

DEVELOPMENT DATA

Monthly Consumer Card fee revenue from Jan 2011 to Dec 2025, together with
macroeconomic data.

In-sample (IS): Jan 2011-Sep 2023; 150 effective regression observations after
transformation and lag construction.
Out-of-time (OOT): Oct 2023-Dec 2025, 27 months.
Production refit: Data through Dec 2025; 177 effective regression observations.

METHODOLOGY

1. Target variable
   Monthly log change in Consumer Card fee revenue:

   y_t = 100 x [ln(Fee_t) - ln(Fee_{t-1})]

2. Model specification
   Linear regression with two lags of the target variable, January and December
   seasonal indicators, and the trailing three-month average of retail sales
   monthly percentage growth.

3. Feature selection
   Screen macroeconomic transformations using IS data, considering statistical
   significance, multicollinearity and residual diagnostics.

4. Performance assessment
   Evaluate Static In-sample, Static OOT and Recursive OOT performance. Assess
   coefficient stability using 60-month rolling windows.

5. Production refit
   After OOT assessment, retain the selected model structure and re-estimate
   the same set of coefficients using all available data through Dec 2025.

6. Scenario forecasts
   Generate 27-month Baseline and S4 adverse stress forecasts from Jan 2026
   to Mar 2028, using fixed production coefficients and a single Dec 2025
   forecast origin. Recursively update lagged target values and convert
   predicted log changes into fee levels. No subsequent actual fee observations
   are used.

ASSUMPTIONS

- Historical relationships between fee growth, lagged fee growth and retail
  sales growth remain applicable over the forecast horizon.
- January and December seasonal effects persist with the estimated magnitudes.
- Baseline and S4 use the same production coefficients and historical starting
  point; differences arise solely from the supplied future retail sales paths.

LIMITATIONS & WEAKNESSES

- Retail sales is the only selected macroeconomic driver; other economic
  transmission channels and changes in pricing, customer mix or payment
  behavior are not explicitly modeled.
- Seasonal indicators impose recurring calendar effects and may not capture
  changes in seasonal patterns.
- Recursive forecasts can accumulate errors over the 27-month horizon.
- Forecasts are conditional point estimates; they do not quantify parameter
  uncertainty or assign probabilities to the macroeconomic scenarios.
